the village

It took a village. None of the rooms in this story ran themselves: behind every conference stood a founder who imagined it, a programmer who built its software, a sysadmin who kept its machines alive, and hosts — named, accountable people — who greeted newcomers, seeded topics, and kept the talk civil enough to continue. These are the main players, in their public capacities, from the sources this edition consulted. It is a roll of the verified, not the complete: for every name here there were staff and hosts no directory printed, and the unnamed are part of the village too.

Frank L. Burns (1939–2003) — founder of The Meta Network (1983) and president of Metasystems Design Group. A retired Army lieutenant colonel — two Vietnam tours, the Silver Star — who had directed Task Force Delta, the Army’s computer-linked network of researchers and futurists, and who is credited with the Army’s “Be All That You Can Be” slogan. Howard Rheingold remembers him as a man who retired “to become a toolmaker for electronic activists.” TMN is item 8’s home; he heads this roll.

Charles Roth — the author of Caucus. A Wayne State computer scientist who encountered CONFER in 1980–82 and wrote its portable heir; later chief technical officer of Screen Porch, which carried Caucus to the web. The item/response grammar this work is written in is, in large part, his design.

Scott Burns — Frank Burns’ son, and, in the editor’s recollection, the system administrator of everything at the Arlington offices: the ISP’s and the Caucus conferences’ servers, in the back room, with Scott living on site. The edition’s own record corroborates his place in the story — the editor’s printed thank-yous, from 2000, begin with his name.

Lisa Kimball — partner in Metasystems Design Group from 1984; founder, in 1985, of the Electronic Networking Association, the consortium TMN helped form; and, decades later, the Caucus community member who interviewed Charles Roth inside a Caucus conference. If one person walks between every room in this story, it is her.

Tom Mandel — president of Screen Porch, quoted in the Caucus 4.0 announcement of 1998 speaking for the web version of the software: the steward who carried Caucus across to the browser.

Robert Parnes — the grandfather of the form: as a University of Michigan graduate student in 1975 he wrote CONFER, the ancestor of Caucus and PicoSpan alike, and founded Advertel Communication Systems to carry it.

Stewart Brand and Larry Brilliant — co-founders of The WELL (1985): Brand, of the Whole Earth Catalog, who set its founding term, “You Own Your Own Words”; Brilliant, the epidemiologist who brought him the idea.

Marcus D. Watts — author of PicoSpan, The WELL’s conferencing software: Caucus’s cousin, from the same CONFER ancestor.

Anne Focke — who conceived ARTS WIRE, proposing it at NYFA’s 1988 Orcas Island gathering, and first led it; and Ted Berger, NYFA’s executive director, who shared the vision and backed it into being.

Anna Couey — ARTS WIRE’s first network coordinator; co-founder, with Judy Malloy, of its Interactive Art Conference, where Scibe was collectively created; co-editor of its first newsletter, Hotwire.

Judy Malloy — ARTS WIRE staff from 1993; editor of its newsletter, Arts Wire Current, from 1996 to 2004; founder of The WELL’s Arts conference; publisher, on The WELL in 1986–87, of the first online hyperfiction — and the orchestrator of My Name is Scibe, the sister work to this one.

Joe Matuzak — Michigan poet; director of ARTS WIRE from 1995.

Ginny Little — the EFA teacher whose TMN classroom conferences neighboured the artist conference; her students found their way in from January 1998 and became the river’s second flood of writers.

Chris Abraham — the editor of this edition: a writer in Scibe on ARTS WIRE and The WELL in 1994, from a desk at PNI (Picture Network International) in the same Arlington building as TMN’s company — adjacent offices and a shared smoking patio, no other connection; founder of item 8 in the artist conference on September 2, 1997.

and the hosts themselves

Above all, the hosts as a class: TMN’s conference hosts, chosen by its moderators, and its conference organizers, the unpaid core with the wider powers; The WELL’s conference hosts; M-Net’s fairwitnesses; ARTS WIRE’s conference coordinators. The record prints few of their names and all of their work: every room in this layer was kept by somebody. The village is mostly them.
