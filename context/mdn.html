MDN

The editor names the company behind The Meta Network as MDN. He gives the identification from inside the building: in 1994 he worked at PNI — Picture Network International, Ltd. — at 2000 North 15th Street in Arlington, Virginia, in the same building as the company’s offices, and he remembers its ground floor, its server room at the back, and Frank Burns’ son Scott at the machines. His name for the company comes from having been in its rooms. In its rooms, but not of them: PNI had no connection of any kind with TMN or its company — the link was the shared building, and the front-entrance patio where the smokers of both offices gathered; by his own account he might have known that world only because of a smoking habit. His professional profile is the documentary anchor for that employment: it dates the PNI work June 1993 to October 1994 — beginning weeks after his May 1993 graduation from George Washington University — in technical customer support for the Seymour platform.

The published record, standing beside his name for it and not reconciled with it, knows the company as Metasystems Design Group — MDG. It is MDG that InfoWorld named on November 4, 1991: “known for its Caucus conferencing software for PC and minicomputer,” with Frank Burns as its president, speaking for “true computer conferencing, such as subject matter indexing.” It is MDG that Howard Rheingold places Burns in as a partner. It is MDG whose principals joined Screen Porch’s founding team in 1996, and MDG whose address the period directories print — 2000 North 15th Street, Suite 103, Arlington, in the later listings; 1401 Wilson Boulevard, Suite 601, in an earlier one.

This entry prints both, because the edition prints what it can stand behind: MDN is the company the editor names, on his ruling and his memory; MDG is the company the documents name. Whether MDN was a name used inside the building, a sister entity, or memory’s compression of the documented initials, no source consulted says. The question is left open here, deliberately — the editor will settle it, and this entry will change when he does. (One nearby name is not this one: “MDN” in this work’s own index is Michelle Diane Nolan, a writer, named by her initials in the text — a different MDN entirely.)
