how this index was made

Every entry was compiled from the transcript itself and then machine-checked against it: for each citation, the cited response was searched for the name or term as the work prints it, and a citation that failed the check was dropped, not argued with. Variant spellings the work itself treats as one thing are grouped — Hope O'Keeffe's two bylines, Dirk Flinthart's joke bylines — as the writers page groups them.

Names are indexed for being mentioned, not for mattering: a name the work speaks once stands here beside a name it speaks forty times, the way they stand in the work. The index records mentions only. It adds no biography, and it identifies nobody the work leaves under a nom de plume — the noms stand as printed.

One honest limit: a common first name is indexed as a person only where the text makes the person plain. Mark is the clearest case — the word is everywhere (a mark of the octopus, a missed mark, a faint white mark); the man is at 8:155, 8:158, and 8:186, and only those are indexed.
